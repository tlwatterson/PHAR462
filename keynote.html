The Keynote Crisis

A demonstration case for learning how Patient Perspectives scenarios work.

You arrive at a national pharmacy conference. Five minutes before the opening keynote, the conference organizer rushes into the room.

"The keynote speaker is missing."

The room contains 1,000 attendees. The event starts in 5 minutes. You have been temporarily placed in charge.

What do you do?

You immediately begin searching for the keynote speaker. After several frantic minutes, you discover:
  • The speaker never checked into the hotel.
  • No one knows where the speaker is.
  • A nationally recognized backup speaker is already onsite.

What now?

You decide to begin the conference on schedule. Attendees seem pleased. Ten minutes later, the keynote speaker walks into the ballroom. Apparently, the speaker had been sitting in the wrong room.

What now?

You ask:

"What do we actually know?"

Within two minutes you learn:
  • No one has called the speaker.
  • No one checked nearby ballrooms.
  • No one reviewed the conference schedule.
A volunteer calls the keynote speaker. The speaker answers immediately.

"I'm in Ballroom C."

Ending: Unexpected Success

You use the backup speaker. The audience loves the presentation. Hours later, the keynote speaker is found at the airport.

Lesson: Good contingency planning can be valuable.

Ending: The Endless Search

You continue searching. The conference starts 30 minutes late. The keynote speaker is eventually discovered in Ballroom C.

Lesson: Action without information can delay solutions.

Ending: Smooth Recovery

The keynote speaker joins the program. The audience appreciates the flexibility.

Lesson: Plans can adapt when new information emerges.

Ending: Sticking to the Plan

The keynote speaker sits in the audience. The backup program continues. Some attendees are disappointed. Others appreciate the consistency.

Lesson: Every decision has tradeoffs.

Ending: Problem Solved

A single question identified the issue. The keynote begins on time.

Lesson: Information gathering can be an intervention.

Ending: Enhanced Experience

Attendees network before the keynote begins. The event starts slightly later, but engagement increases.

Lesson: Understanding the situation can create opportunities.
